Narrowing the ANN–SNN Gap for Continuous 1D Temporal Signal Classification with Multi-Scale Temporal Encoding and Sparsity-Regularized Transform Encoding

Sun, Qi, Huang, Yulei, Cao, Zhen, Hou, Biao

Multi-scale temporal encoding enriches short-horizon evidence, while sparse transform encoding controls redundant firing for continuous 1D signal classification.

Spiking neural network Continuous 1D signals Multi-scale temporal encoding Sparse transform coding

Three-Minute Brief

Multi-scale temporal encoding enriches short-horizon evidence, while sparse transform encoding controls redundant firing for continuous 1D signal classification.

Core Claim

With matched backbones and training budgets, the mean ANN–SNN accuracy gap is modest on continuous 1D signals; complementary MTE and STE encoding further improves average performance.

Method Overview

MTE constructs multi-scale streams with scale-aligned integer spikes. STE learns spike codes through an analysis transform, training-only reconstruction, and sparsity regularization. Reconstruction branches are removed at inference.

Why It Matters

The framework connects input information density with internal firing redundancy and can be inserted into multiple backbone families for modulation, fault diagnosis, and physiological signal tasks.

Key Contributions
  • Analyzes short-horizon ANN–SNN differences through temporal evidence accumulation.
  • Combines denser multi-scale input evidence with sparsity-regularized transform codes.
  • Reports matched comparisons, component ablations, and efficiency estimates across ten datasets.
Reading Note

Read Figure 2 before comparing backbone averages and task-specific outcomes in Table 1. The 77.5% result is an aggregate, not superiority over ANNs on every dataset.
