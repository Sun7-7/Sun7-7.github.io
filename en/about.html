About

Qi Sun

Researcher in Neuromorphic Computing, Signal Processing, and Semiconductor Device Modeling · School of Artificial Intelligence, Xidian University

My research centers on neuromorphic computing and asks how spiking neural networks can remain stable under noise, low-power constraints, and complex temporal structure. Radar and remote sensing are application settings for neuromorphic signal processing, while semiconductor device modeling is a physically constrained extension of data-driven modeling.

Neuromorphic Computing Signal Applications Device Modeling
Research Core

Neuromorphic mechanisms

SNN training stability, surrogate gradients, membrane-potential distributions, and temporal coding.

Applications

Signal-processing tasks

Radar pulse sequences and remote-sensing images are application testbeds for neuromorphic models.

Extension

Device-aware modeling

TCAD data, physical priors, and ML surrogates for power-device optimization.

Research Identity

I position my work along a continuum: neuromorphic computing methods, signal-processing applications, and intelligent semiconductor device modeling. The central question is not only accuracy, but reliability under noise, sparse temporal representations, low latency, and physical constraints.

Radar and remote sensing are best understood here as application domains of neuromorphic computing: they provide complex signals, real noise, and low-power requirements for evaluating SNNs and event-driven representations.

Academic Training

  • School of Artificial Intelligence, Xidian University, with systematic training in pattern recognition, neural networks, deep learning, intelligent target recognition, and AI chip design.
  • Undergraduate background in intelligent science and technology, connecting algorithms, machine learning, electronic circuits, and device fundamentals.

Method Capabilities

  • Method design and experimental validation for SNN direct training, surrogate gradients, temporal encoding, knowledge distillation, and event-driven computing.
  • Neuromorphic signal-processing pipelines for radar PDW sequences and remote-sensing images, including sorting, denoising, recognition, deinterleaving, and low-step inference.
  • ML-assisted optimization of LDMOS, U-MOSFET, and related power device structures using TCAD data and physical priors.

Publication Footprint

This site currently includes 8 papers across AAAI, ICASSP, IEEE Signal Processing Letters, Neurocomputing, IEEE Transactions on Electron Devices, and Micromachines.

These works map onto three lines: SNN and neuromorphic mechanisms, neuromorphic applications in radar/remote-sensing signal tasks, and ML-assisted device modeling and optimization.

Keywords

Spiking Neural Networks, Neuromorphic Computing, Temporal Encoding, Radar Signal Processing, Remote Sensing SNNs, Signal Deinterleaving, Surrogate Function Optimization, TCAD, LDMOS.
