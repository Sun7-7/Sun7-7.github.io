Research

I study how spiking networks represent and learn continuous temporal information, with applications to radar pulse streams and related signal tasks. My device research addresses the relationship between structure, electric fields, and electrical behavior, alongside interests in neuromorphic device modeling and design.

01

Spiking Learning & Temporal Computing

My work on short-horizon learning in continuous signals addresses two complementary questions: preserving temporal structure at the input and aligning surrogate gradients with neural activity during training.

MTE×STE, presented at ICML, combines multi-scale input streams with sparse transform encoding. MPO, presented at AAAI, adapts surrogate gradients to membrane-potential distributions. They address representation and gradient optimization respectively, with separate experimental analyses.

02

Radar & Intelligent Signal Processing

My radar research studies deinterleaving without labels, encoding and recognition under noise, and cluster decisions across scenarios, using distinct learning mechanisms to recover pulse structure with limited supervision.

Spiking-SOM moves competitive learning and topological organization into the spike domain. SGTE encodes reference similarity as temporal events. The IEEE SPL study combines adaptive clustering with similarity learning for cluster decisions. Related applications include remote sensing and multimodal sensing.

03

Semiconductor Devices & Neuromorphic Hardware

I investigate power-device design and modeling through the relationships among geometry, electric fields, and model fidelity. A separate interest concerns how device behavior informs neuromorphic models and cross-layer constraints.

Semiconductor Device Design & Modeling

Published studies in APL, IEEE TED, and Micromachines address dielectric trenches, field management, and data-driven models, using the TCAD analyses, surrogate predictions, and device experiments reported in each paper.

Neuromorphic Devices & Cross-layer Design

This separate research interest concerns device models, neuron dynamics, neuron-array modeling, and the relationship between algorithmic and hardware constraints.
